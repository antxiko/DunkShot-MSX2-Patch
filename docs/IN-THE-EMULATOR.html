In the emulator

Everything on this site was measured in openMSX: the original cartridge on an MSX1, the patched one on an MSX1 and the patched one on a real MSX2, the Philips NMS 8250. Nobody has run the patch on real hardware yet.

The probe

tools/omsx_parpadeo.tcl reads, every frame, the attribute table the VDP is showing (register 5, and register 0 to know which sprite mode is on) and counts, line by line, how many sprites fall on each one. Those above the VDP's limit, four on the TMS9918 and eight on the V9938 in sprite mode 2, are the ones that are not drawn. It adds them up per frame and, at the end, dumps the VRAM, the VDP registers and a screenshot. tools/lanza_parpadeo.sh launches it:

sh tools/lanza_parpadeo.sh <dir> <machine> <rom> <limit> <from> <to> "<keys>"

The keys go as second row mask of the keyboard matrix: space is row 8 with 0x01, down is row 8 with 0x40. Space three times goes from the title to the menu, to SET-UP and to the match, with the computer playing both sides. The MSX2 boots later, hence the later keys. With DS_THROTTLE=on it runs at real speed, to watch it.

On the MSX1 the patched cartridge behaves like the original: register 5 alternates 0x7C and 0x7E, 899 frames each, and the count is that of a different match, not of a worse game. On the MSX2, register 0 is 0x04 and register 5 is 0x7F in all 1,505 frames.

Dunk Shot was published by HAL Laboratory for the MSX in 1986; it is 32 KB. All rights in the game remain with their holders. This is an unofficial patch: only the difference file is distributed here, never the cartridge image.
